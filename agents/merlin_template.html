      Every feature is computed for each of the 55 instruments from its own price (PX) and from market-wide series: the Nasdaq-100, VIX, the 10-year yield, Brent, WTI, gold and the 10-year note. IC here is the correlation between the feature and the stock's return over the next 5 days in excess of the Nasdaq-100, pooled across stocks, so it rewards telling winners from losers. For the index, oil, gold and bond futures the plain return is used. Raw price levels (moving averages of the price, price ratios) drift over time, so they count for about a third. Features must keep the same sign in both halves of the training period, and near-duplicates (over 80% alike) are dropped.

        What this is. A learning demo on real daily closes, not investment advice. Risk controls apply to every model: a stop-loss per position (exit and skip it for 5 days at the STOP % loss), a drawdown cut (halve the book for 10 days after it falls DD CUT % from its peak), position sizes scaled to each instrument's volatility, at most SECTOR MAX positions per sector, slippage that grows with volatility, and a monthly roll cost on oil, gold and bond futures. Merlin is one Q-learning agent shared by all 55 instruments. Under MODEL you choose its value function: Linear (a weighted sum of the features, easy to explain) Deep (DQN), a small neural network with two hidden layers of 32 units, replay memory and a target network; kNN, which follows what happened after the most similar past days of the same instrument; or k-furthest, an experimental contrarian that bets against what happened after the least similar days. Prices start in January 2016; stocks that listed later, such as ARM or PLTR, join from their first trading day: the Nasdaq-100 index (through NQ futures or QQQ), the 50 largest members by market cap today, and front-month futures on Brent crude, WTI crude, gold and the US 10-year Treasury note. Futures prices are Yahoo Finance's continuous front-month series, so they jump a little at each monthly roll, and quantities are shown in contracts on a US$1 million book. Each day it scores every instrument as short, flat or long, keeps up to the chosen number of the strongest calls, and gives each a fixed slot of the book (10% with the default of 10 positions; unused slots stay in cash). Positions it already holds get priority to stay, so it does not churn the book when the ranking shuffles. It pays the cost you set on every change of weight. It trains on the first 62% of days, picks its checkpoint on the next 18%, and is scored on the last 20%. The universe is today's 50 largest members, so the history has survivorship bias: stocks that shrank or left the index are not in it, which flatters any long strategy. Stock prices are adjusted closes from Yahoo Finance.
