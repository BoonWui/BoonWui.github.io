Palm Oil Fundamentals

Malaysian stocks reached a new record of 3.62 Mt in October (MPOB, 10 November), up from 3.51 Mt, in line with this report's 3.61 Mt estimate; output held at 2.15 Mt, near September's record. That caps prices into year-end. But exports are surging in October, India has cut duties, and a strong El Niño is forming, which usually cuts output 6–12 months later. Near term looks heavy; 2027 looks tighter.

What changed since the 23 October report

  • October output stayed high. An industry survey puts the full month at −0.8% on September, with individual planters ranging from −5% to +10%. That means about 2.10 Mt, not the 2.00 Mt I had.
  • The export surge slowed in the last days of the month. For 1–25 October, two cargo surveyors show +47% and +68% on September, down from +59% and +78% at day 20. October exports now look like about 1.69 Mt, down from 1.77 Mt.
  • October stocks are now likely a new record of about 3.61 Mt, against 3.43 Mt in the last report. This lowers the price paths by about 5%.
  • FCPO peaked at RM4,595 on 20 October and eased to RM4,433 by 2 November. The October average was RM4,439.
  • Indonesia's November export tax drops. The estimated reference price is $979 (bracket 7), down from $1,042. Levy plus duty on CPO falls from about $308/t to $246/t, which makes Indonesian oil cheaper against Malaysia's.
  • Still in place: El Niño at 1.4 with a 62% chance of a strong event by October–December. India's 11% CPO duty since 24 September.

Weather and supply capacity

El Niño brings drier weather to Malaysia and Indonesia. Palm trees respond with fewer fruit bunches 6–12 months later.

El Niño index (ONI), °C

Three-month seasons. Above 0.5 is El Niño; above 1.5 is strong. The forecast gives 89% odds of at least 1.5 by October–December.

The last strong El Niño, Malaysia production, Mt

After the 2015 event, 2016 output fell 13%, and 18–25% in March–May.

Malaysia replanting, thousand hectares a year

Replanted trees take about 3 years to bear fruit. 2025's jump to 191k ha trims supply in 2026–28.

Supply drivers at a glance

  • Mature area is shrinking: 5.04 M ha in 2025, down from 5.14 M ha in 2021. Immature area rose to 657k ha.
  • Yields peaked in September: fresh fruit yield 1.88 t/ha and CPO yield 0.38 t/ha, the highest in the file.
  • Costs are rising: 2026 estates cost about RM8,144/ha in Malaysia (+14%) and $1,085/ha in Indonesia (+17%), mainly fertiliser.
  • Indonesia's B50 mandate needs 16.75 M kL of biodiesel in 2026 (B40 used 15.62 M kL in 2025), and 20.1 M kL at full B50.

Buyers: India and China

India is the swing buyer and has just been helped by a duty cut ahead of Diwali (8 November). China is still sitting on heavy stocks.

Import margin, US$ per tonne

Below zero, importing costs more than the local price pays. India uses the 11% CPO duty from 24 September (5% basic duty, 5% farm cess and a 10% surcharge on both) and adds 5% GST to the landed cost; if Kandla local prices are quoted before GST, India's margin would be about US$70 per tonne higher. China adds 9% duty and 9% VAT, matching local prices that include VAT.
