Strategy Test Lab

Pick a market and as many trading methods as you like, then run 13 tests on each: a backtest, purged cross-validation, walk-forward, an untouched forward test, the probability of backtest overfitting, the deflated Sharpe ratio, a bootstrap, a random-timing test, cost stress, settings stability, market regimes, other markets and buy-and-hold. Settings and the winning method are chosen on the development years only; the last 20% of days stays untouched until the end. Everything runs in your browser on real daily closes. A learning tool, not trading advice.

How the tests avoid overfitting

  • Development and forward-test split. The first 80% of days is the development period; the last 20% is the forward test, used once, at the end, for reporting only. It never chooses a setting or a method.
  • Purged cross-validation (5 folds, with the forecast horizon removed on both sides of each test fold so no label leaks across) chooses each method’s setting and ranks the methods.
  • Walk-forward: re-fit on the last 500 days, choose the setting inside that window only, trade the next 63 days, roll on, and stitch the out-of-sample days together. Walk-forward efficiency = walk-forward Sharpe ÷ in-window Sharpe.
  • Probability of backtest overfitting (PBO) by combinatorially symmetric cross-validation (Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado and Zhu): the development period is cut into 8 blocks; for all 70 ways of choosing 4 to “train” and 4 to “test”, how often does the best setting in training land in the bottom half in testing?
  • Deflated Sharpe ratio (Bailey and López de Prado): the chance the walk-forward Sharpe is real after allowing for every setting of every selected method that was tried, and for fat tails.
  • Bootstrap, random timing, cost stress, settings stability, regimes, other markets and buy-and-hold check that the result is not luck, not cost-sensitive, not a single lucky setting, not one market phase and not one market.
  • Machine-learning methods use 16 features known at each close (returns over 1–120 days, distance from 20/50/200-day averages, RSI, volatility, 55-day range position, and the other markets’ recent moves), standardised on training days only, to forecast the move over the chosen horizon. Rules are fixed formulas with a few settings each. Positions are taken at the close and earn the next day’s move; every change in position pays the cost.

Data: FCPO third-month daily closes from Sina Finance (since 2020), CBOT soybean oil, Brent and USD/MYR from Yahoo Finance (since 2019), all from this site’s data files. Past results don’t predict future returns. Not financial advice.
