El Niño and the output outlook

El Niño brings drier weather to Southeast Asia, and palm trees respond with fewer fruit bunches 6–12 months later. Because that lag is long, today's index already says something about output into next year.

Sources and method

Prices: World Bank Commodity Price Data (Pink Sheet), CC BY 4.0. El Niño: NOAA Climate Prediction Center, Oceanic Niño Index. Malaysia: Malaysian Palm Oil Board (MPOB) monthly supply and demand. Scorecard rules: momentum = 3-month price change beyond ±5%; palm–soy and palm–crude spreads compared with their 5-year middle half; El Niño = the ONI 9 months ago at or above +1.0 (bullish) or at or below −1.0 (bearish); stocks = stock-to-usage against the 5-year average for that calendar month (±15%); valuation = market premium to the model beyond ±15%. Valuation model: ordinary least squares of log price on stock-to-usage and a linear time trend, monthly since 2014. El Niño projection: least squares of production y/y on the ONI 9 months earlier since 2015. The data is rebuilt by a GitHub Actions job on the 5th and 20th of each month. For research and education only. Not trading advice.
