Rules to trade it by

  • Enter when the 7-day average funding, as a yearly rate, is above your entry level and the yield on your capital beats your cash yield by enough to pay the fees back within 2 weeks. Open both legs within a minute of each other: buy the coin on spot, short the same quantity of the USDT perpetual.
  • Hold while the 3-day average stays above your exit level. Funding changes every few hours; one negative payment is normal, a run of them is the signal.
  • Exit when the 3-day average drops below the exit level: close both legs together, again within a minute.
  • Keep the short safe. Use cross margin with leverage of 2× or less and top up margin (or trim both legs) whenever price has risen 25% since entry. A liquidated short leaves you holding the coin unhedged — the only way this trade loses big.
  • Spread across two exchanges and no more than half your crypto capital on any one; exchange failure (FTX, 2022) is the main risk the test can’t show.
  • Prefer liquid coins. Small coins often pay high funding because they are about to crash, can be delisted, and their spot and perp prices can drift apart when you exit.

How the numbers are worked out

  • Yearly funding = average funding per payment × payments per year (8-hour contracts pay 1,095 times a year, 4-hour 2,190, 1-hour 8,760).
  • Yield on capital. Only the short earns funding, but both legs need money. With separate accounts and 2× leverage, $1 of position needs $1 for the coin plus $0.50 of futures margin, so capital earns 1 ÷ 1.5 = 67% of the funding rate. A unified account that counts the coin as margin needs about 10% extra, so 91%.
  • Fees. Taker: 0.10% spot + 0.05% futures per side, 0.30% of the position to open and close. Maker: 0.08% + 0.02% per side, 0.20% round trip. Your VIP level or exchange token can lower this.
  • The test uses each coin’s Binance funding history, enters and exits only on information known at the time, charges fees on every entry and exit, and earns your cash yield while out. It ignores the small gap between spot and perp prices at entry and exit, borrowing costs (none for this trade), and exchange failure.
  • What to expect. In 2021–2026 the majors paid 5–8% a year on average, more in bull markets and close to zero in bear markets. It is a steady, low-risk yield when funding is high, not a way to get rich; when funding is low the right answer is to wait in cash.
