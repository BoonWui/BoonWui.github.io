Palm Import Margins

Do importers in India and China make money on a new cargo of palm oil today? The margin is the local price minus the full cost of bringing oil in: the cargo price, import duty, VAT and port costs. When margins are positive, buying tends to pick up, which supports palm oil prices; when they are negative, buying slows.

India’s duty and tariff value

India charges duty on a government “tariff value”, not on the price paid. The value is reset about every two weeks and the duty rate when policy changes. Both are read every day from SEA India.

How it is worked out

India (crude palm oil): value = (CPO C&F Mumbai + duty rate × tariff value) × USD/INR; landed cost = value + ₹1,203 port handling + 5% VAT on (value + port). Margin = CPO ex-Kandla price − landed cost, shown in US$. Prices: SEA India daily quotes; duty and tariff value: SEA’s duty and tariff PDFs; USD/INR: ECB reference rates.

China (refined olein): CNF = RBD palm olein FOB + about US$20 freight (an estimate); cost = CNF × 1.09 (import duty) × 1.09 (VAT) + ¥100 port handling. Margin = Dalian palm oil main contract − cost, in US$. Dalian is a futures price; physical prices at Chinese ports usually trade a little below it.

Europe and other buyers: not shown. There is no free daily price for palm oil delivered in Rotterdam or elsewhere, so a real margin can’t be worked out from public data.
