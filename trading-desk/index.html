What’s due

    House rules

    • Follow the plan, not the news. Each plan’s edge comes from doing the same thing every time. Overriding a signal because of a headline turns a tested plan into a guess.
    • No leverage on top. Put-writing is cash-secured, the carry is hedged one-for-one, the ETF plan is fully paid. Borrowing to do more is how steady plans blow up.
    • Kill switches. Stop a plan and review it if its losses pass 1.5 times the worst drawdown in its test: about −27% for ETF Trend, −33% for cash-secured puts, or any liquidation warning on a carry.
    • Real money in steps. Start each plan at a quarter of its target size for the first month, half for the second, full from the third, so execution mistakes are cheap.
    • Crypto Vol stays on paper until its go-live gate opens. In a 768-window study the model was no better than Polymarket’s own prices.
    • Keep a log. Every order, its price and the reason. Compare your fills with each page’s test once a month.
