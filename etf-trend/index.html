Trading it from Malaysia

The signals are worked out on the US-listed ETFs because they have the longest price histories. You can trade the same indices through Ireland-registered (UCITS) funds on the London Stock Exchange, which cuts the US dividend tax from 30% to 15% and avoids US estate tax: a non-US person holding more than $60,000 of US-registered assets, US ETFs included, can face US estate tax of up to 40%.

How it works and what to expect

  • The rule. At each month-end, score each ETF by the average of its 1, 3, 6 and 12-month total returns. Hold the top N equally weighted, but only those with a positive score; the rest of the money goes into SHY (1–3 year Treasuries, close to cash). Trade on the first day of the next month.
  • The trend filter option instead holds every ETF that closed above its 10-month average, each at 1/10 of the account, the rest in SHY. It earns less but falls much less.
  • Why it should keep working. Trend-following across asset classes has worked in decades of data and in many markets (Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen, 2012; Faber, 2007; Antonacci, 2014). It doesn’t need to predict anything: it steps aside from long falls such as 2008 and 2022 and stays in long rises.
  • What it won’t do. It lags in sharp V-shaped rebounds, and it trails the S&P 500 in long bull markets. Expect several flat or losing years in 20. About 1 in 5 rolling 12-month periods lost money in the test.
  • The test is honest about timing. Each month’s holdings use only month-end prices known at the time, returns include dividends, every change is charged the trading cost you set, and cash earns the SHY return. The test starts when all 10 ETFs exist (2007); before then not all asset classes were tradable.
  • Tax. Malaysian residents holding US ETFs pay 30% US withholding tax on dividends, which this test does not deduct (it lowers returns by roughly 0.3–0.6% a year). Ireland-domiciled UCITS versions of the same indices cut that to 15%.
